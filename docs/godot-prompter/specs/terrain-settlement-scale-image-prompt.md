# Settlement scale progression image prompt

Built-in image generation prompt used for
`terrain-settlement-scale-concept.png`.

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: game world planning concept board

Primary request: Create one wide, four-panel environment concept board that
demonstrates how the same faux-2.5D world grows from a single route stop into a
village, a town, and a city without shrinking the player, roads, doors, houses,
trees, or terrain cells. This is a scale and composition study for a Godot
exploration game, not a beauty shot and not a literal runtime screenshot.

Input images:
- Image 1: gameplay-scale elevation reference. Preserve its readable stepped
  terrain grammar, house/player proportions, warm stylized construction, cliff
  faces, stairs, broad paths, water, and high oblique faux-2.5D viewpoint.
- Image 2: macro terrain and settlement reference. Use only its landform,
  district, terrace, water, and settlement-density ideas. Simplify its visual
  detail so the four scales remain readable.

Scene/backdrop: Four equal panels arranged left to right on a restrained neutral
planning-board background. Each panel depicts the same temperate river-valley
region at a progressively wider camera extent while keeping every human-scale
object at the same physical proportions.

Panel progression:
1. Route stop / homestead: one lodge parcel, one to three small support
   structures, a broad trail, water edge, field or garden, and one clear
   interaction loop. Lots of breathing room.
2. Village: eight to twenty full-size buildings clustered around a commons,
   trail junction, water source, and one strong landmark. Show roughly four
   connected neighborhood terrain chunks with low, middle, and upper terraces.
3. Town: thirty to eighty full-size building masses organized into a market
   street, civic or service core, residential edges, farms, and two to four
   distinct neighborhoods. Show roads, stairs, a broad ramp, bridge, and clear
   district boundaries across a larger chunk group.
4. City: several town-scale districts connected by gates, bridges, stairs,
   ramps, and major roads. Show a civic high terrace, dense market district,
   residential terraces, an outer agricultural edge, and waterways. The city
   should visibly continue beyond one active neighborhood, implying streamed
   district groups rather than one always-loaded scene.

Style/medium: polished stylized environment design board; tactile painted
3D/2.5D forms with restrained voxel-like modular construction; original game
world, not based on any existing franchise. Clear enough to reason about chunk
extent, routes, terrain elevation, biome zones, and settlement density.

Composition/framing: consistent high oblique camera angle in every panel. The
camera pulls farther back from panel to panel, but include a same-size scale key
inside every panel: one player silhouette beside one standard lodge-sized
building and one road segment. Use subtle panel dividers. Keep the main road
network and elevation connections visually traceable.

Lighting/mood: soft warm daytime light, exploratory and welcoming, moderate
contrast, minimal atmospheric haze so topology stays legible.

Color palette: moss and meadow greens, warm ochre roads, gray-brown cliff stone,
clear teal water, cream plaster, red or teal roofs; biome variation becomes more
visible at larger scales without becoming noisy.

Materials/textures: readable grass tops, layered cliff faces, compact earth,
stone stairs, wooden bridges, plaster and timber buildings, cultivated fields,
wetland vegetation, and highland trees. Surfaces should be calmer and less
individually blocky than the references.

Text: no labels, captions, legends, numbers, logos, or watermarks. The document
will supply the panel names separately.

Constraints:
- Exactly four panels and exactly the four requested settlement scales.
- Preserve a consistent world scale across all panels; do not miniaturize later
  buildings or roads merely to fit more structures.
- Every panel must show traversable elevation through stairs, ramps, or bridges;
  cliff edges without a connector must read as blocked.
- Settlements follow terrain and water instead of sitting on one flat plane.
- Roads, entrances, commons, plazas, and gates remain generous enough for the
  full-size player scale.
- The city is composed of recognizable districts, not an undifferentiated carpet
  of tiny houses.
- No grid overlay, UI chrome, minimap, terrain editor interface, annotations,
  floating islands, fantasy megastructures, or extreme mountains.
- No recognizable copyrighted characters, creatures, buildings, symbols, or
  franchise styling.
```
